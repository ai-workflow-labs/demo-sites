Should You Sell or Rent Your Miami Home?

By Eve Runciman, Florida-licensed attorney and real estate professional. Updated November 2026. Draft for Eve’s review.

Short answerCompare what you would keep after selling with what you would keep each year after renting. Sell if the net proceeds would do more for you elsewhere, or if renting would cost you a tax benefit. Rent if the home covers its own costs and you are comfortable being a landlord.

What to compare

Three rules that change the math in Florida

Homestead

Renting the entire dwelling for more than 30 days per calendar year for 2 consecutive years counts as abandoning the homestead under section 196.061, Florida Statutes.

The Save Our Homes cap

On a homestead, the annual rise in assessed value is capped at 3% or the change in the consumer price index, whichever is lower. A sale resets the assessment, though part of the saving may be portable to a new Florida homestead.

Capital gains

Federal law lets many owners exclude up to $250,000 of gain, or $500,000 for joint filers, if the home was their main home for 2 of the 5 years before the sale. Renting for too long can close that window.

If you own a condo

Check the association documents first. Leasing limits, approval requirements, monthly fees and special assessments can all change the result.

Common questions

Can I rent out my Florida homestead and keep the exemption?

Short rentals may be fine. Under section 196.061, renting the entire dwelling for more than 30 days per calendar year for 2 consecutive years counts as abandoning the homestead.

Does renting my home affect capital gains tax when I sell?

It can. The federal exclusion generally requires the home to have been your main home for 2 of the 5 years before the sale.

Can a condo association stop me from renting my unit?

Association documents can limit leasing, for example with minimum lease terms or approval requirements. Read them before you count on rental income.
